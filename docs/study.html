Study this project

For someone who wants to learn from this project rather than follow it: what to read in which order, the ideas you need, the words we use, how to run it yourself, and a few exercises.

Reading order

  1. Overview: the question (can self-play from nothing beat a network that started as a copy?) and the stages. 5 minutes.
  2. Curriculum: the three stages and what ends each. 3 minutes.
  3. The model: where the learning signal comes from when the opponent is itself; the "What it buys" figure. 15 minutes.
  4. Training: the score against the GA genome and the bots, which it never trains on in stage 1, and the game length and entropy charts. 5 minutes.
  5. Play: watch iteration 0, early self-play and the newest network on the same game. 10 minutes.
  6. The code: train.py (STAGES, pick_opponent, ppo_update), rl/jobs.py (gae: the advantage in 12 lines), c/rl.c (features, forward pass, sampling). 1 hour.

Key concepts

Self-play

The network plays against a copy of itself. The opponent is always exactly as strong as the learner, so every game is informative: never hopeless, never trivially won. Here: stage 1, 80% of the games (both seats learn). What people get wrong: a rising score against itself means nothing (it is always about 50%); progress has to be measured against fixed opponents it does not train on, here the GA genome and the bots.

Starting from random (tabula rasa)

No examples and no rules: the weights are random, every legal choice is about equally likely. Here: bc_games = 0, iteration 0. What people get wrong: it is not hopeless. In a random game the side that happens to buy more money and Provinces still wins more often, and that small bias is enough signal to start.

Policy gradient

Change the network so that the choices in games that went well become more likely. The "how well" is the advantage. Here: ppo_update. What people get wrong: it does not know which of a game's 40 decisions won it; only averaging over many games sorts good decisions from lucky ones, which is why it needs millions of games.

Advantage and GAE

The advantage of a decision is how much better things went than the network itself expected at that moment. GAE builds it from the network's own win estimates along the game (λ = 0.95). Here: gae() in rl/jobs.py. What people get wrong: with a bad win estimate the advantages are bad too; the value head matters even though it never makes a decision.

PPO's clip

One update may change the probability of a recorded choice by at most ±20%; beyond that the gradient stops. It keeps learning from noisy data from destroying a good policy in one step. Where: clip, max_kl in CFG; the "PPO step size" chart on Training.

Exploration by sampling, evaluation by argmax

In training the network draws its choices at random from its probabilities; in every measurement it takes its top choice. What people get wrong: comparing the training games' win rate (with exploration) with the evaluation (without).

A league of opponents

Opponents from several sources so the policy stays good against all of them, not just the latest. Here: stage 1 only past selves (20%); stage 3 everything: the GA genome, the bots, az-v1, rl-v1. What people get wrong: pure self-play can cycle (A beats B beats C beats A) without getting better.

Reproduce it

8-core laptop CPU (i5-8250U), 8 GB RAM, Linux, Python 3.14, gcc:

git clone --recursive https://github.com/JCA374/dominion-rl-v2 && cd dominion-rl-v2
python3 -m venv .venv && .venv/bin/pip install torch --index-url https://download.pytorch.org/whl/cpu
.venv/bin/pip install numpy pytest pillow matplotlib
.venv/bin/python -m pytest -q tests        # builds rl.so; "4 passed"
.venv/bin/python -u train.py --run mine --set games=1500   # from random; ~14 s per iteration at first
.venv/bin/python experiments/goal_check.py runs/mine/net_25.npy --name mine25 --vs_az   # ~4 min

Needs about 2 GB of free memory: the first iterations' random games are long (1,500 games ≈ 300,000 decisions × 298 inputs).

Exercises

  1. Easy: watch it learn to buy. Run 20 iterations and draw the "What it buys" figure (site_hooks.fig_buys). When do Curses disappear? When does Province overtake Duchy?
  2. Medium: no past selves. Change stage 1's shares to {} (only the current net) and compare the evaluation against the GA genome after 100 iterations. Does it cycle?
  3. Medium: exploration. ent=0 versus ent=0.03: watch the entropy chart. From random, too little entropy can lock in an early habit.
  4. Hard: is the teacher a ceiling? Compare this run's best with rl-v1's at the same number of games, and head to head (experiments/goal_check.py with an rl-v1 net as the opponent).
