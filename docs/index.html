Learning Dominion from nothing, by playing against itself

The fourth method for the Dominion project. A network that starts random, with no examples, no hand-written rules and no imitation, and learns every decision of the game (buys, actions, trashing, gaining, discarding) only from the results of games against itself. Only later does it meet the bots and the earlier models. The question: how far does pure self-play get on its own, and can it end up better than rl-v1, which started by copying the evolved GA genome and now scores 82.8% against it and 71.7% head to head against az-v1?

What it plays right now

What this is

Dominion is a deck-building card game: every turn you play action cards from your hand, then buy cards for your deck; the player with the most victory points when the game ends wins. A game has about 40 real decisions per player, of about 20 kinds.

Where it starts. At iteration 0 the network's weights are random numbers, so each legal choice is about equally likely. It buys Curses as happily as Provinces, plays nothing, buys nothing. Against the GA genome and the bots it loses every game. A random game takes about 26 turns per player and is almost always ended by the turn limit or by three empty piles, not by the Provinces running out.

How it gets better with no teacher. Two copies of the same network play each other. One of them wins, usually because by chance it bought a few more Silvers, Golds or Provinces. Every decision the winner made becomes a little more likely, every decision the loser made a little less. Repeat for thousands of games, and "buy the thing that wins" emerges: first money and Provinces, then which actions are worth playing. Because the opponent is always itself, it is always an even match: never too easy, never hopeless, which is what makes self-play work.

The danger. A player that only ever meets itself can get stuck: it learns to beat its own habits, not to play well, and can go round in circles (A beats B beats C beats A). Two defences: 20% of its games are against its own past snapshots (so it may not forget how to beat old versions), and it is measured after every update against opponents it never trains on in stage 1: the GA genome and the three bots. That measurement is how you can see, on this site, whether self-play is learning Dominion or only learning to beat itself.

What came before. dominion-v1 evolved priority lists with a genetic algorithm (the "GA genome"). az-v1 replaced the genome's buys with an AlphaZero-style search (70% against the genome). rl-v1 is this project's parent: the same network and the same PPO, but it started as a copy of the genome (imitation), then improved by reinforcement learning against a league. rl-v2 changes only the start: no imitation, self-play first.

Approach

Stage 1, self-play. Each iteration plays 1,500 games: 80% the network against itself (both seats learn), 20% against one of its last 8 snapshots (taken every 10 iterations). The stage runs until the measured score against the GA genome has not set a new best for 60 iterations: self-play has stopped improving by itself (at most 400 iterations). Stage 2, the bots. 35% of games against Big Money, Smithy-BM and Witch-BM, until it beats them 70% of the time (at most 400 iterations). Stage 3, the league. Every earlier model: the GA genome, az-v1's network and its search, and rl-v1's networks, plus the bots and its past selves.

How one update works (PPO). The network samples its choices from its own probabilities, so it tries things. After the games, each decision gets an advantage: how much better the game ended than the network's own win estimate expected at that moment. Choices with a positive advantage are made more likely, negative ones less, and PPO caps how far one update may move the policy, so one lucky batch cannot wreck it.

Real numbers (8-core laptop CPU, no GPU): 151,331 weights (298 inputs → 256 → 256 → 34 choices + 1 win estimate), the same network as rl-v1, running in C inside the game engine. Early random games are long and slow (about 14 s per iteration); once it plays sensibly an iteration takes 5–10 s.
