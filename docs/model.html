The model

The same network as rl-v1, started from random weights instead of from a copy of the GA genome. Everything it knows comes from games against itself (stage 1), then the bots and the earlier models.

The network

 298 inputs (what I know + the question)
   │  dense 298 → 256, ReLU
   │  dense 256 → 256, ReLU
   ├──▶ 34 logits: a card id (or a Sentry option) or PASS; illegal answers masked out → softmax → a choice
   └──▶ 1 number: tanh → my win estimate in [−1, +1]
 151,331 weights; one forward pass ≈ 20 µs in C

One small multilayer perceptron for every kind of decision: the "question" inputs say what is being asked (a buy, which action to play, what to trash, …). Playing to win it takes the highest-scoring legal answer; in training it samples one in proportion to its probabilities, which is how it explores.

Iteration 0: a random player

The weights are drawn at random (PyTorch's default initialisation, seed 0), so the 34 logits are all close to 0 and every legal answer is about equally likely: entropy 1.37 (in nats; it falls as the network becomes sure of itself). Such a player buys Copper, Curse, Estate and Province with equal enthusiasm, plays actions at random and often buys nothing. Its win estimate is noise. It loses every game against the GA genome and the bots.

Stage 1: self-play

The games. Each iteration plays 1,500 games. In 80% both seats are the current network, and both seats' decisions are training data (twice the data per game). In 20% the opponent is one of its last 8 snapshots (one every 10 iterations) and only the learner's decisions count.

Where the signal comes from. Two random players are not equal in every game: the one that happened to buy more Silver and Gold, or more Provinces, wins more often. PPO makes the winner's decisions more likely. After a few iterations both copies buy money and victory cards a bit more, so the game gets harder, so the next small edge (Gold over Silver at 6 coins, Province at 8, not buying Curses, Militia, Witch) becomes what decides games. The opponent improves at exactly the rate the learner does: the curriculum is automatic.

The advantage. For every decision: how much better did the game go than the network's own win estimate expected at that moment (GAE, λ = 0.95, from the final +1/−1)? In a random start the win estimate knows nothing, so the first iterations are almost pure "did this game win": a very noisy signal (one result per ~40 decisions), averaged over ~150,000 decisions per iteration.

The update. PPO: decisions with positive advantage become more likely, negative ones less, by at most ±20% in probability ratio (the clip), 3 passes over the data, stopped early if the policy moved too far (KL > 0.03). A small entropy bonus (0.01) keeps it trying alternatives.

How you know it learns Dominion, not just "beat myself". After every update it plays 400 games against the GA genome and 300 against the bots, which it has never seen in stage 1. If self-play only learned its own quirks, those scores would stall or fall while its score against its past snapshots kept rising. The "What it buys" figure below shows what it has actually learned to buy.

When it ends. When the score against the GA genome has not set a new best for 60 iterations: self-play has stopped improving by itself. Then the bots come in (stage 2) and later every earlier model (stage 3).

Why start from zero at all?

rl-v1 started as a copy of the GA genome. That is fast, but the copy brings the genome's habits along, and PPO only moves away from them where a small change wins more often. A network that never saw the genome has no habits to unlearn; it may find a different, better style, or it may get stuck in a worse one. This is the AlphaGo Zero question on a laptop: is the teacher a head start or a ceiling?
